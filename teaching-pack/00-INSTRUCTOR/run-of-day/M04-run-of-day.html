Run of day · 75 minutes

00–08 · The one-number brief

Take the vote; withhold the verdict.

SLIDE

Student slides 1–3 — title, Diane’s ask, FOREMAN’s 5,004 psi adequacy sentence.

SAY

“The lab sent back cores_2027.csv: 24 Otter Bend deck cores, each with a location and a compressive strength. Diane has one line in a county briefing and wants one number. FOREMAN filled that line with the mean. Is this ready to repeat?”

COLLECT

M03 HW2 source-check work before opening the new inbox.

HAND OUT

H4-01 and H4-03. Take ready / not ready / unsure without endorsing any group.

WATCH FOR

FOREMAN’s mean is intentionally visible and correct. The gated answer is what that mean omits.

08–23 · Center and spread

Three summaries, three jobs.

SLIDE

Student slides 4–8 — correct-but-incomplete, variables, mean, variance, and SD.

SAY

“One row is one tested core. The mean tells us where those strengths balance. Variance records squared distance from that balance; standard deviation brings the spread back to psi. Twenty-four values can share a center and still tell very different stories.”

DO

Make students say the units: mean in psi, variance in psi², SD back in psi.

WATCH FOR

Do not calculate the class file aloud. Use an unrelated three-number example if arithmetic support is needed.

23–33 · Relative spread and scope

CV, then sample versus population.

SLIDE

Student slides 9–10 — CV and .S versus .P.

SAY

“Standard deviation tells us how many psi of spread. CV normalizes that spread against the mean, so variability can be compared across scales; it is not a universal pass line. And .S versus .P is not a software preference—it is a claim about whether these 24 cores sample something larger.”

DO

Ask why 24 rows can be the entire CSV but still only a sample of the deck.

WATCH FOR

Do not call STDEV.P wrong in every context. It answers a different population claim.

33–55 · Spreadsheet hunt

Require center, spread, count, and scope.

SLIDE

Student slide 11 — activity. Move to slide 12 for pair reports.

SAY

“Work in groups. Calculate the mean, sample standard deviation, and coefficient of variation. Then choose the single number you would use to summarize center and the single number you would use to summarize spread. Write two numerically correct sentences: one from FOREMAN’s number alone, and one that puts variation back. Be ready to explain which Diane can safely repeat.”

HAND OUT

H4-02 and cores_2027.csv.

DO

Pairs compute AVERAGE, VAR.S, STDEV.S, CV, MIN, MAX, and COUNTIF. Fast pairs inspect drill_zone.

WATCH FOR

If formulas disagree, ask which range they selected and whether the header entered the range. Do not announce values.

55–67 · Reveal the compressed story

The mean remains correct.

SLIDE

Reveal slides 1–5 — all values, statistics table, two sentences, descriptive uncertainty, shoulder-only sample.

SAY

“Now the reveal. The mean is 5,003.75 psi. Sample SD is 554.67 psi; CV is 11.09%; five of 24 results are below 4,500 psi. The spread introduces uncertainty that the center cannot carry. FOREMAN did not calculate badly—it made one statistic carry an adequacy claim it cannot support.”

DO

Ask what drill_zone = shoulder leaves unobserved. Separate descriptive results from any code-compliance determination.

WATCH FOR

Never teach “one low core means failure” or “mean above threshold means adequate.” The project value is a comparison, not a complete acceptance standard.

67–73 · Write the usable answer

Short, qualified, reproducible.

SLIDE

Reveal slide 6 only after students draft their own response.

SAY

“Present the finding in three sentences. Sentence one gives Diane the requested center. Sentence two gives SD or CV and the low-result count, and says why variation matters. Sentence three names what this sample can—and cannot—establish about the whole deck. Then name the check you actually performed.”

HAND OUT

H4-04 three-sentence brief.

DO

Students write independently. Accept equivalent wording that preserves all three jobs and avoids an engineering disposition.

73–75 · Mask off

Diane needs brevity, not false certainty.

SLIDE

Reveal slides 7–8 — mask-off and before Tuesday.

SAY

“Diane is wrong if ‘one number’ means ‘erase the spread,’ and right that a client needs a short answer. The mean, SD, CV, and sample scope answer different questions. Diane is not the grader. Your reasoning, check, and record are.”

DO

Collect H4-04 and have students add the six-hour decision to their semester log.
