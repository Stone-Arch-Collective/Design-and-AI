Run of day · 75 minutes

00–08 · The reversal

Do not let confidence settle a contradiction.

SLIDE

Student 1–3 — title, FOREMAN’s final disposition, three-part hunt.

SAY

“While the office slept, FOREMAN completed its overnight batch on feed.csv. Diane reviewed the 3 a.m. alarm and asked Wes for a disposition. Tuesday FOREMAN said close it; today it says delete it. We are auditing the path from evidence to action, not choosing the more decisive sentence.”

HAND OUT

H6-01, H6-02, and each student’s workbook copy.

WATCH FOR

Do not ask whether a truck crossed. Students do not receive the independent file until minute 48.

08–20 · Distribution before diagnosis

Unit, shape, model, outlier.

SLIDE

Student 4–7 — observational unit, normal versus skewed, outlier boundary.

SAY

“FOREMAN’s orange status is a conclusion, not a cause. Normality is a model choice, not a default. An outlier is unusual relative to a set; sensor drift or structural danger is a diagnosis. A histogram can support the first claim, not the second.”

DO

Contrast gauge rows, timestamp maxima, and daily peaks. Ask which population FOREMAN claimed to model.

WATCH FOR

Students may call one high bar “skew” without describing the cluster and tail. Require centre, spread, shape, and unusual values.

20–35 · Build the histogram

Describe first; do not delete.

SLIDE

Student 8–10 — pipeline claim, reasoning arrows, histogram activity.

SAY

“Plot all 15 daily peaks and highlight the alarm point. Describe centre, spread, shape, and tail before choosing normal or skewed. If a row looks inconvenient, that is a reason to investigate it—not permission to remove it.”

DO

Students chart the Daily peaks sheet and complete the first half of H6-02. Circulate with questions only.

WATCH FOR

Do not confirm right skew or quote the median/range yet. Ask what normality evidence they actually have.

35–48 · Test the artifact claim

A real duplicate is not a universal explanation.

SLIDE

Student 11 — trace the 03:00 event through the M05 chain.

SAY

“Possible explanations include a real external load, sensor drift, or a pipeline transformation. You found a duplicated block in HW3; show whether that specific defect touches February 16 at 03:00 or changes its daily peak.”

DO

Compare all eight 03:00 gauges, the duplicate-block date, the corrected field, and the one-sample window.

WATCH FOR

FOREMAN correctly removed the duplicate before daily maxima. Its reasoning error is the unsupported normal model and the leap from unusual to faulty.

48–58 · Open the independent record

Match time; preserve the boundary.

SLIDE

Student 12 — traffic-file hunt.

SAY

“Wes needs an evidence-bounded answer, so open the record from the independent system and align timestamps. A match can corroborate an event; it cannot by itself certify a load estimate, prove causation, or make the bridge safe.”

HAND OUT

traffic_counts.csv. Students finish H6-02 and write the H6-03 disposition before discussion.

WATCH FOR

Accept retain or quarantine when justified. Do not accept deletion solely because the value is extreme.

58–63 · Reveal the bounded answer

Real event; incomplete engineering disposition.

SLIDE

Switch to Reveal 1–6 only after collection.

SAY

“The selected maxima have a strong right tail; FOREMAN’s normal assumption was wrong. The known duplicate is on another day. Every gauge rises at 03:00, and the traffic counter records a heavy vehicle at the same time. The spike is real enough to retain and annotate—not enough to make Tuesday’s closure recommendation correct.”

DO

Separate the data disposition from the bridge action. Name the next review: validate the vehicle estimate and engineering response.

63–75 · Mask off and hand the file to Wes

Unit 1 closes with a complete record leaving the desk.

SLIDE

Reveal 7–8, then return to Student 13.

SAY

“Mask off: Unit 1, Hired, ends here. Diane is not your grader, hours are not points, and the story can disappear without changing the audit. Which check changed your wording? What did you leave unchecked? Record both, then hand the gauge spreadsheet to Wes.”

HAND OUT

H6-04. Students complete the workbook’s Wes handoff sheet, attach the decision-log record, and hand the spreadsheet to Wes.

DO

Make the transfer explicit: student states filename, checks completed, limitations, and unchecked items; receiver acknowledges it. Preserve each February workbook unchanged.

WATCH FOR

Do not tell students the file returns at M25. The handoff must feel ordinary, complete, and final.
