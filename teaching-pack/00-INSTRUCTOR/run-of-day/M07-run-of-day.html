Run of day · 75 minutes

00–10 · Inbox

The all-clear arrives under schedule pressure.

SLIDE

Student 1–3 — title, deadline, FOREMAN/Diane inbox, three-part hunt.

SAY · CANON

Diane: “The report is in. No defects found. High confidence. Let’s move.” Wes: “I’m drafting the memo to accept the machinery rating based on the vendor summary.” Student: “Wait, I need to check the training data source first.” Diane: “It looked at 400 photos in a minute. Give me a reason not to use it.”

HAND OUT

FOREMAN-M07-damage-screen and WES-M07-accepted-screen-memo first. Ask students to underline every sentence that sounds like evidence.

WATCH FOR

Do not let students treat Diane’s approval or FOREMAN’s confidence as validation.

10–35 · Teach

Bias, representation, shift, and domain.

SLIDE

Student 4–9 — definitions and the four comparison dimensions.

SAY · CANON

“Read the vendor summary. Where were these photos taken?” Expected: “Warm states, highway girders only.” Ask: “What does Otter Bend have that this data never saw?” Expected: “Lift machinery, freeze and thaw, road salt.” Then teach representative sample, training-data bias, distribution shift, out-of-domain use, and coverage gap.

DO

Distinguish shift from proof that every rating is wrong. Distinguish confidence from a detector of ignorance. State: “The AI rates the machinery room with the same confidence as the girder photos. It has no sign of uncertainty because it was never trained on them.”

WATCH FOR

Require the phrase “representative of what?” Large sample size alone is not a coverage argument.

35–47 · Lab A

Read the metric’s population.

SLIDE

Student 10 — profile the archive.

SAY · CANON

“Open H7-01. Underline the population behind 93.8 percent. Record bridge configurations, climate, labels, and evaluation split. Circle what is absent or not recorded.”

HAND OUT

H7-01, H7-02, H7-05, and H7-06. Keep the key and reveal closed.

WATCH FOR

Students may call the random split fraudulent. It is not; it supports a narrower same-archive claim.

47–60 · Lab B

Map the 12 photos: match, shift, or gap.

SLIDE

Student 11 — Otter Bend mapping.

SAY · CANON

“Compare the 12 photos against the training summary distribution. Mark photos where the site context differs. List lift machinery, freeze–thaw, and road salt. Note whether confidence falls when the domain shifts.”

HAND OUT

H7-03, H7-04, and the CSV. Students complete both columns of H7-05.

WATCH FOR

Do not confirm the 4/8 grouping. Ask which disclosed category each component can map to.

60–65 · Lab C + reveal

Commit the boundary; reveal the designed error.

SLIDE

Student 12–13 — recommendation and note v2.

SAY · CANON

“State what the evaluation score honestly supports. Choose which ratings remain provisional and which require review.” After students commit: “FOREMAN assigned high confidence to out-of-domain machinery images because its workflow did not flag missing coverage.”

DO

Students commit H7-05 before discussion. Switch to reveal slides 1–6. Accept more than one bounded action when the evidence trail is explicit.

WATCH FOR

Blanket rejection is not the goal. Strong answers preserve only the use the evidence can support.

65–75 · Note v2 + close

Professional judgment records the coverage gap.

SLIDE

Reveal 7–8, then Student 13–14.

SAY · CANON

“Write Note version 2: the claim, the check, and what the check showed. Professional judgment overrides automated confidence when the training record does not cover the use.” Close lightly: “The conditions around a bridge do not stay fixed through the year.”

DO

Collect H7-06 only. Preserve Wes’s accept-memo as liability color within M13’s existing messy unit-mismatch handoff.

WATCH FOR

Do not name temperature-as-damage or turn M13 into a memo-return scene.
