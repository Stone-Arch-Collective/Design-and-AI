Run of day · 75 minutes

00–08 · Wes’s draft

Read §4.7 aloud and keep moving.

SLIDE

Slides 1–3 — title, FOREMAN summary, county engineer stakes.

SAY

“Wes moved this into the interim report this morning. Ten requirements, each with a section number so we can find the source. The county engineer wrote the source.” Read the §4.7 row in passing. Do not emphasize it.

HAND OUT

H3-02 FOREMAN summary only. Keep KC-MB-12 face down.

DO

Ask whether the summary looks ready to send. Take two reactions and move on.

WATCH FOR

If someone catches §4.7 immediately, ask only: “What would prove that?” Do not confirm.

08–20 · Weighted dice

Likely is not certain.

SLIDE

Slides 4–6 — mechanism, probability, one roll.

SAY

“A language model is a weighted die rolled several hundred times to make the paragraph in front of you. The output can read well without consulting the bridge.”

DO

Keep probability concrete: fair die, weighted die, 0 to 1, likely versus certain.

WATCH FOR

Do not over-teach probability. This block exists to make the activity legible.

20–30 · Become the model

Roll once; then take the widest column.

SLIDE

Slides 7–9 — activity, comparison, truth questions.

SAY

“Round one: roll. Round two: never roll—always choose the widest option. Same table, one changed setting.”

HAND OUT

H3-03 dice activity, one per pair.

DO

Run both eight-word rounds. Read three fluent nonsense sentences. Ask whether any consulted Otter Bend or the specification.

WATCH FOR

Round two loops: THE DECK IS WITHIN TOLERANCE AND THE DECK… That is useful, not a failure of the activity.

30–45 · Scale the table

Tokens, prediction, temperature, prompts.

SLIDE

Slide 10 — the mechanism at scale.

SAY

“A prompt is simply the text that conditions the next roll. Notice what is still missing from this list: any step where the model opens the document.”

DO

Tie tokens to words, prediction to rows, temperature to rolling, and prompt to “everything so far.” If licensed, run one short prompt twice; cut without loss if not.

WATCH FOR

Do not say “hallucination.” Not yet.

45–65 · Open the source

Check whether it is there, not whether it sounds right.

SLIDE

Slides 11–12 — section break and source-check instructions. Leave slide 12 showing.

SAY

“One partner holds the source, one holds FOREMAN. Take all ten in order. A plausible sentence gets no credit unless you can point to it.”

HAND OUT

H3-01 county specification and H3-04 worksheet.

DO

Circulate. Do not confirm §4.7 until a pair says the section does not exist. At minute 58 prompt: “Section 4 ends where?”

WATCH FOR

Eight correct claims are deliberate. Students must not stop after finding one error. Ask fast pairs what is absent from the summary: §2.4.

65–72 · Land both errors

First the missing section; then the changed word.

SLIDE

Slides 13–18 — §4.7 question and reveal, §3.2, verdict table, text-shaped source, then HALLUCINATION.

SAY

“Did anybody find Section 4.7?” Wait. “It does not exist. And in §3.2, ‘shall review and seal’ became ‘should be reviewed.’ One citation was invented; one obligation was softened. Now you get the word: hallucination.”

DO

Ask who benefits from §4.7 being real: it licenses FOREMAN’s own 92% deck shortcut from M01.

WATCH FOR

Correct “it lied” gently: lying requires knowing the truth. The system generated text shaped like the source; nothing malfunctioned.

72–75 · How to catch it

Source first; confidence never.

SLIDE

Slides 19–21 — checking practices, mask-off, before Thursday.

SAY

“Open the source. Check that the citation exists before checking what it says. Confidence is not a check: FOREMAN was 96% sure.”

HAND OUT

H3-05 HW2.

DO

Assign all ten claims, corrections, the §4.7 callback to M01, and note v1.

WATCH FOR

Mask off: KC-MB-12 is invented so students can legally read it in full. The error types are real.

“If §4.7 is real, find it and I’ll stop asking.”Diane · the source, not the model, gets the last word
