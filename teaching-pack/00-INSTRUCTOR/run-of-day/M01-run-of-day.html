Run of day · 75 minutes

00–10 · The hire

Open the job; do not resolve the contradiction.

SLIDE

M01 deck, slides 1–8 — first day, cast, Otter Bend, then FOREMAN’s demo and Diane’s memo.

SAY

“You were hired this morning. Kinnick County wants one answer by May: rehabilitate this bridge, or replace it. Before nine o’clock, two things arrived—and they do not agree.”

HAND OUT

H1-01 orientation memo. Hold every other handout.

DO

Put FOREMAN’s 6.8 / 92% result on screen, then read Diane’s “how do you know?” line. Ask what it would take to check either claim.

WATCH FOR

If students call FOREMAN wrong, say: “We have not checked it yet. Fast and uncheckable is not the same as wrong.”

10–25 · A rule

Make every line visible.

SLIDE

Slides 9–11 — Rules; formula; worked Girder G-3.

SAY

“The arithmetic is not the lesson. The lesson is that a stranger can redo it. Nobody has to trust Diane.”

HAND OUT

H1-02 load-rating sheet.

DO

Work G-3 slowly on the board: RF 1.716; 61.8 tons. Name each source value.

WATCH FOR

Impact belongs on the moving live load, not dead load. Correct the placement, not merely the arithmetic.

25–35 · Their rule

Students work G-4 and write a check.

SLIDE

Slides 12–13 — activity prompt, then answer.

SAY

“Show every substitution. Then write one sentence that says how you know—not who told you.”

DO

Give 7 minutes before revealing slide 13. Answer: RF 1.692; 60.9 tons. Accept RF 1.69–1.70.

WATCH FOR

Do not turn passing RF into an unconditional bridge decision. This is one simplified check on one girder.

35–50 · A learned pattern

Two sources look at the same six panels.

SLIDE

Slides 14–16 — learned patterns, training, ratings chart.

SAY

“Nobody wrote a spalling formula. FOREMAN learned from 41,800 labeled photographs. Now compare its eyes with Diane’s field memory.”

HAND OUT

H1-03 FOREMAN assessment and H1-04 panel history, one set per pair.

DO

Pairs compare panel-by-panel and circle the largest disagreement without explaining it yet.

WATCH FOR

The chart compares different years. Do not let “difference” silently become “model error.” Ask what each source could know.

50–57 · D-15 lands

The highest confidence carries the largest error.

SLIDE

Slides 17–19 — confidence question, D-15 reveal, “Nobody here is wrong.”

SAY

“Which panel was FOREMAN most confident about?” Wait. “D-15. The deck was overlaid in 2018. The photograph shows the overlay, not the deck.”

DO

Let silence do the work before advancing to slide 19.

WATCH FOR

A “better model” still cannot infer hidden material from a surface photo. The limit is the input and question, not a malfunction.

57–69 · Sort the work

Rules, patterns, and the unresolved ninth card.

SLIDE

Slides 20–23 — comparison, activity, answers, M03 setup.

SAY

“If your pair agrees on all nine, argue harder about number 9.”

HAND OUT

H1-05 task sort.

DO

Debrief items 8 and 9 only. Land #9: looking up an interval is a rule if you open the document.

WATCH FOR

Refuse to explain what happens when a language model “looks it up.” Say M03 will return to it and stop.

69–75 · The note

One sentence; then the mask comes off.

SLIDE

Slides 24–26 — note template, mask-off, before Thursday.

SAY

“Diane is a character. She is not your grader. Your note is graded on whether the check was real, not whether the answer later proved right.”

HAND OUT

H1-06 note v1 + rubric. Collect the completed note before dismissal.

DO

Read one strong and one weak note anonymously if time permits. Tell students to bring a ruler or calipers Thursday if they own one.

WATCH FOR

Do not assign HW1 yet. It arrives after the M02 reveal.
