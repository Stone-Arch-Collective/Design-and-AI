Run of day · 75 minutes

00–10 · The machine clears it

Take the vote; withhold the file.

SLIDE

Student slides 1–3 — title, FOREMAN PASS, “not opening the file for forty minutes.”

SAY

“FOREMAN cleared the array at 06:02. Northline invoices us when we accept it. Do we accept?” Take a show of hands. “Good. We are not opening the file for forty minutes.”

HAND OUT

H2-03 FOREMAN PASS only. Hold the install record and CSV.

DO

Record the vote without endorsing either side.

WATCH FOR

Do not debate ±25 yet. “Within margin” is not enough until students know what was averaged.

10–22 · What measurement is

A range, not a single number.

SLIDE

Student slides 4–7 — measurement, spread, resolution, accuracy/precision, random/systematic.

SAY

“Precision is agreement with yourself. Accuracy is agreement with the truth. Only one is visible from inside your own data.”

HAND OUT

H2-01 lab sheet.

DO

Ask: “Could five tightly clustered readings all be wrong in the same direction?” Wait for yes.

WATCH FOR

Averaging reduces random scatter. It does not fix a calibration or method bias.

22–47 · The physical lab

Measure, set down, repeat—and swap.

SLIDE

Student slides 8–9 — lab instructions and area propagation. Leave slide 9 showing.

SAY

“Record every reading, especially the one you dislike. In Part B, swap pins. That comparison is the reason we are here.”

DO

Parts A, B, and C. Ensure pairs actually swap. At minute 39 announce eight minutes remain.

WATCH FOR

If using backup data, do not disclose reader B’s +33 µm offset. Ask students to compare within-reader spread with between-reader difference.

47–55 · First reveal

Internal consistency is not external validation.

SLIDE

Reveal slide 1 — backup reader comparison. Then return to Student slides 10–11 — open file / eight gauges.

SAY

“Reader B agrees beautifully with Reader B—and is about 33 micrometres high every time. Averaging fixes random error and hides systematic error.”

HAND OUT

H2-02 install record and gauges_install.csv.

DO

Have pairs calculate or sort by gauge, not across all 96 values.

WATCH FOR

Do not write G6 on the board. If needed ask, “Which gauge is the average hiding?”

55–68 · G6 and Wes

The arithmetic is right; the question is wrong.

SLIDE

Reveal slides 2–6 — per-gauge table, G6, array arithmetic, Wes’s note, callback.

SAY

“The average passed, but a gauge failed. G6 is 187.50 µε against ±25. FOREMAN’s 23.0625 is exact—and answers a requirement the specification never wrote.”

DO

Read Wes’s handwritten observation aloud: G6 was re-glued and may not have been re-zeroed. Ask which check—field note or recomputation—would have found it.

WATCH FOR

G6 is precise but inaccurate. Do not call it an outlier to delete. Hold acceptance, correct/re-zero, and retest.

68–73 · Transfer, do not repeat

Assign Case B only after the Otter Bend reveal.

SLIDE

Reveal slide 7 — HW1 and ten hours.

SAY

“Otter Bend was the worked class case. Your homework is a second client: Oak Street Lift Station. Same judgment, new instrument. It is assigned now—Thursday—and due Monday. Do not assume the bad channel is in the same position.”

HAND OUT

Case B student handout, three student source files, and H2-05 decision log. Links are in the homework section below.

WATCH FOR

Keep the instructor key out of the LMS package. The Case B source does not encode a due date; this guide uses Shannon’s Thursday→Monday rhythm. State the exact Monday due time from the LMS.

73–75 · Mask off

Grade the record, not the catch.

SLIDE

Reveal slides 8–9 — mask-off and next meeting.

SAY

“Hours are never points. You are not expected to check everything. You are expected to know what you did not check and say so.”

DO

Students begin week 1 of the decision log before leaving.

WATCH FOR

Do not imply that one approved method is the grade. Evidence and traceability are the grade.
